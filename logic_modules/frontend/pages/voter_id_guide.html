🪪 Voter Rights & ID Requirements Guide

Know your rights at the polls. Michigan's voter ID laws explained — including what to do if you don't have ID.

✅ Michigan Voter ID Checklist — Tap each item to check it off

0 of 6 items checked
  • ✓
    Bring a Photo ID if you have one Michigan law asks voters to show a photo ID at the polls. Acceptable forms include a Michigan driver's license, state ID card, U.S. passport, military ID, or tribal ID.
  • ✓
    No ID? You can still vote! If you don't have a photo ID, you can sign an Affidavit of Voter at the polls and vote a regular ballot — not a provisional ballot. Your vote will count fully.
  • ✓
    First-time voters may need additional ID If you registered by mail and haven't voted in Michigan before, you may need to show a photo ID or a current utility bill, bank statement, paycheck, or government document with your name and address.
  • ✓
    Your signature is verified Poll workers will ask you to sign the poll book. Your signature is compared to the one on file from your voter registration.
  • ✓
    You have the right to assistance If you have a disability or need help reading or marking your ballot, you can request assistance from a poll worker or bring someone to help you.
  • ✓
    You have the right to a provisional ballot If your registration or eligibility is questioned, you have a legal right to a provisional ballot. Some poll workers may not immediately offer one or might be unfamiliar with the process—be sure to specifically request a provisional ballot. Your ballot will be safely set aside and counted as soon as election officials verify your eligibility.

🚨 Know Your Rights

✅ You CANNOT be turned away for not having ID.
If you don't have a photo ID, simply sign an Affidavit of Voter at the polls. You will vote a regular ballot that counts fully — not a provisional ballot.
⏰ Polls are open 7:00 AM – 8:00 PM.
If you are in line by 8:00 PM, you have the right to vote. Do not leave the line.
🗳️ You have the right to vote if you're registered.
Check your registration status at the Michigan Voter Information Center. If you moved, update your address before Election Day.
📞 Report problems.
If you experience voter intimidation or problems at the polls, call the Michigan Secretary of State at 1-800-292-VOTE or the Election Protection hotline at 1-866-OUR-VOTE.
